###————————————————————————–### ### CONTEXT NOTE FOR READER ### ### Why the IPL Matters — For Non-Cricket Audiences ### ###————————————————————————–###

The Indian Premier League (IPL) is the world’s most valuable cricket

franchise tournament, generating over USD 15 billion in brand value

(Duff & Phelps, 2023) and attracting the highest-paid cricketers globally.

Established in 2008 by the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI),

it operates as a Twenty20 (T20) league — the shortest and most commercially

dominant format of the sport, where each team faces exactly 20 overs

(120 legal deliveries) per innings.

WHY IT MATTERS GLOBALLY:

─────────────────────────

1. SCALE: The IPL is the second most-attended sporting league in the world

by average attendance per game, behind only the NFL (ESPN, 2023).

The 2023 IPL final alone drew 130 million television viewers in India.

2. FINANCIAL DOMINANCE: Media rights for the 2023–2027 cycle were sold

for USD 6.2 billion — surpassing the per-match value of the English

Premier League. Every major cricket board now models its domestic

T20 league on the IPL structure.

3. TALENT CONCENTRATION: All 10 IPL franchises actively recruit from

every cricket-playing nation. Players from Australia (BBL), Pakistan

(PSL), West Indies (CPL), South Africa (SA20) and England (The Hundred)

compete in the IPL — making it the de facto global standard-setter

for T20 cricket strategy, rules, and innovation.

4. RULE-MAKING POWER: Because of its financial and cultural dominance,

what the BCCI does in the IPL influences cricket globally. When the

IPL introduced the Impact Player rule in 2023, other leagues immediately

began debating whether to adopt it. The ICC (International Cricket

Council) is watching closely.

WHY THE IMPACT PLAYER RULE IS A GLOBAL POLICY QUESTION:

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The Impact Player rule allows teams to substitute one player mid-match

for tactical — not injury — reasons. A team may replace a struggling

batter with a specialist, or bring in an extra bowler after assessing

match conditions. This is unprecedented in cricket’s history and

structurally analogous to the designated hitter rule in MLB — a

substitution mechanic that fundamentally altered team composition,

player valuation, and strategic approach.

Unlike baseball’s DH rule which took decades to be universally adopted,

the IPL’s global reach means the Impact Player rule could reshape

cricket’s player development pipeline — specifically the value placed

on all-rounders (players who both bat and bowl at a high level) —

within a single generation of players.

The BCCI’s post-IPL 2026 review therefore carries implications not just

for Indian domestic cricket but for how the next generation of cricketers

worldwide are identified, developed, and valued at auction.

WHAT THIS PAPER CONTRIBUTES:

──────────────────────────────

This is the first causal analysis of the Impact Player rule using

ball-by-ball data across four T20 leagues (IPL, BBL, PSL, CPL) and

four outcome dimensions (run rate, wicket-taking, competitive balance,

all-rounder deployment). The timing is deliberate — the BCCI review

begins immediately after IPL 2026 concludes, and this paper provides

the empirical evidence base that policy discussion currently lacks.
